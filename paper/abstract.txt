Electrons above 2 MeV at geostationary orbit charge spacecraft dielectrics, and forecasts are needed also at longitudes that no GOES satellite monitors. We compare STORM-PhysNet, a forecast network with a learned solar-wind delay, a gate driven by the southward interplanetary magnetic field, outputs relative to persistence and a physics-informed loss, with 22 neural and two classical models. All forecast 1, 6, 12 and 24 h ahead from hourly GOES-15 data (2011--2020), with ten random seeds on three disjoint test periods, a protocol that prevents information leakage and paired t-tests. Predicting the change from persistence gives the 1 h skill, and solar-wind inputs give the 6--24 h skill. The delay, the gate, two alternative gates, a spectral head, magnetopause features and the physics loss evaluated on standardised inputs change no score significantly. With a long short-term memory (LSTM) encoder, STORM-PhysNet reaches prediction efficiencies of 0.975, 0.852, 0.790 and 0.725, and a plain LSTM predicting the change from persistence matches it within 0.007. Both are among the most robust to noisy and missing solar-wind data. A ridge regression on summaries of the same window is as accurate on clean inputs and transfers best to GOES-16 and GOES-18, but loses its skill under input noise. Models that forecast the flux level directly lose their skill on GOES-16, whose flux reads a factor of two lower than GOES-15, whereas models that forecast the change keep part of it. With local data, fine-tuning brings all models to similar skill on GOES-16, GOES-18 and India's GSAT-19.
