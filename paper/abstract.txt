Electrons above 2 MeV at geostationary orbit charge spacecraft dielectrics, and forecasts are needed at longitudes that no GOES satellite monitors. We compare STORM-PhysNet, a network with a learned solar-wind delay, a gate driven by southward interplanetary magnetic field, outputs relative to persistence and a physics-informed loss, with 28 neural and two classical models. All forecast 1, 6, 12 and 24 h ahead from hourly GOES-15 data (2011–2020), with ten seeds on three disjoint test periods, leakage-free splits and paired t-tests. Predicting the change from persistence gives the 1 h skill; solar-wind inputs give the 6–24 h skill. The delay, the gate, two alternative gates, a spectral head, magnetopause features and the physics loss change no score significantly, even when the physics terms act at full weight throughout. With a long short-term memory (LSTM) encoder, STORM-PhysNet reaches prediction efficiencies of 0.975, 0.852, 0.790 and 0.725, matched within 0.007 by a plain residual LSTM. Gradient-boosted trees on window summaries are more accurate (0.978, 0.864, 0.813 and 0.743). A longer training schedule, tested post hoc, raises STORM-PhysNet by 0.045 at 6–12 h, past its LSTM variant, and lets a residual Transformer match the trees there. The trees have the lowest storm-time error and are the most robust to noise; the LSTM models keep more skill without solar-wind data. Level-forecasting models lose their skill on GOES-16, whose flux reads half that of GOES-15; change-forecasting models keep part of it. Local fine-tuning brings all networks to similar skill on GOES-16, GOES-18 and India's GSAT-19.
