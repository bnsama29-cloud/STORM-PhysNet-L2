Electrons above 2 MeV at geostationary orbit charge spacecraft dielectrics, and forecasts are needed also at longitudes that no GOES satellite monitors. We compare STORM-PhysNet, a forecast network with a learned solar-wind delay, a gate driven by the southward interplanetary magnetic field, outputs relative to persistence and a physics-informed loss, with 18 other model families. All are trained on hourly GOES-15 data (2011--2020) to forecast 1, 6, 12 and 24 h ahead, with ten random seeds on each of three disjoint test periods and a protocol that prevents information leakage; differences are tested with paired t-tests. Predicting the change from persistence gives the 1 h skill, and solar-wind inputs give the 6--24 h skill. The delay, the gate, two alternative gates, a spectral head, magnetopause features and the physics loss, as applied to standardised rather than physical inputs, change no score significantly. The encoder matters more: with a long short-term memory (LSTM) encoder, STORM-PhysNet has the highest 1 h skill of all models (prediction efficiency 0.975), matches the best at 6 h (0.852), is the most robust to input noise and missing solar-wind data, and transfers best without retraining to GOES-16 and GOES-18, at a cost of 0.016 at 24 h. Models that forecast the flux level directly lose their skill on GOES-16, whose flux reads a factor of two lower than GOES-15, whereas models that forecast the change keep part of it. With local data, fine-tuning brings all models to similar skill on GOES-16, GOES-18 and India's GSAT-19.
